A little about me

The person behind the classroom.

My background combines psychology, special education, teaching in France and international classrooms in Bangkok and Singapore. These experiences have made me attentive to different starting points, while reinforcing my belief in clear structure, thoughtful planning and high expectations.

I enjoy creating learning experiences in which French has a real purpose — telling a story, preparing a performance, discovering another culture or contributing to a wider school event. I want students to understand what they are learning, see their own progress and, above all, develop the desire to keep learning.

03

Inquiry & Assessment

Planning for different learners.

A prepared Grade 1/2 Carnaval inquiry with different language supports around shared questions: what do we notice, how do celebrations compare, and how can we explain a choice?

IB PYP · Differentiation · Assessment

Explore planning — Inquiry and Assessment

Context

A prepared Grade 1/2 Carnaval inquiry, connecting cultural celebrations with the PYP concepts of form, connection and perspective.

Learning sequence

Observe celebrations in Nice and Québec, compare details, explore masks and music, create a response, then explain and reflect.

Curriculum connections

IB PYP inquiry and British Columbia French language learning.

Different entry points

The plan uses photographs, modelled phrases and choices between images. Beginning speakers can point, name a detail or dictate to an adult. More confident speakers can compare or explain with « parce que… ».

Evidence to look for

Look for a detail noticed, a comparison made or a choice explained. Note the support used before deciding on the next step. Limited French should not automatically be mistaken for limited understanding.

This is a planning sample, not a claim of completed implementation or measured student outcomes. Original school documents and assessment records are not published.
